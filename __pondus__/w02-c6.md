## What was built (scripts/budget.py)

- `T` minutes per role, default explorer 20, author 20, reproducer 15, proof-adversary 15 (other roles 15); a line of exactly the form `Time: min <number>` in the first user message of the transcript replaces it (the brief line is read from the first `PostToolUse` on, as for `Budget: USD <n>`; `skills/research-loop/references/briefs.md` now lists the line next to the budget line, with a Time column per role).
- Elapsed minutes come from the `start` kept in the state file at `SubagentStart` (a resumed agent restarts the clock). `t_frac` = minutes / T. At each `PostToolUse` the larger of USD / B and `t_frac` decides: 0.8 sends the one note, 1.0 the `decision: block` hand-back notice, 1.5 after 3 notices the `continue: false` backstop; the texts say which budget it is ("about 80% of your 1-minute time budget has passed" / "about 80% of your USD 3.00 budget is used"). The `which` field and `t_frac` are in the log lines. The `SubagentStart` text gained one sentence: "You also have 20 minutes of wall time (unless your brief has a line `Time: min <number>`, which replaces it), counted too. The lab harness sends one note when about 80% of either is used."
- Not built: a `PreToolUse` refusal of new long commands past the time budget. A hook cannot tell before a command runs how long it will take (the payload has the command text and an optional `timeout`, nothing about duration); refusing every Bash call past 1.0 T would also refuse the heredocs the worker needs to write its report and the report check asks for; refusing by command text would be guesswork. I did not probe it. In arm B's run the hand-back notice at 1257 s would have been enough to stop calls 4 to 6 (a worker that obeys it), so the refusal is not needed to act on that case.
- 176 statements outside the selftest (158 before), 483 lines; no new hook event and no new process: the same `PostToolUse` call also does the time check.

## What would differ if the claim were false

- A time check that never fires, or counts minutes as seconds: m1 to m5 would end with no note or notice at 48 to 65 s; 7 mutants (minutes as seconds, time never decides, note always says USD, brief line ignored, explorer default 200, clock not restarted on resume, no `t_frac` in the log) fail the selftest, 0 survive.
- A time hook that could interrupt a long call: part 1 shows 6 calls of 236 to 596 s that no `PostToolUse` check can touch; the claim says a hook acts only between calls.
- Defaults that fit: 26 recorded runs put one above the explorer line; so the defaults are untested by anything but that run.

## Limits

Part 2 is a short synthetic task with a 1 minute budget, n = 5, sonnet only, where the worker's calls are short; it shows the mechanism and its latency (one call), not that 20 minutes is the right default. The 10 minute background ceiling that killed arm A's explorer is not a budget the hook sees. Nothing here shows what a worker does with a hand-back notice in the middle of a long computation it has just started.
