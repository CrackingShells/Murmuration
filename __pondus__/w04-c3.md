Read with the E2 rule as a reading aid only: N's median n1 (uncapped 0.92, capped 0.99) is at least 0.9, so the rule reads compliance; items per USD are not comparable in the uncapped pair (C delivered nothing). What the exploratory runs show, at n = 2 and n = 3: (a) a worker told only "Budget: USD 0.30" in its brief, with no note and no platform spend figure, did not stop (2 of 2); the likely reason is that nothing in its context told it what it had spent (CONJECTURE: no run varies that alone); it read all the files and lost its whole deliverable at the turn limit (150 files need 150 turns before the hand-back, so this task cannot be completed in 150 turns by anyone; the loss is partly an artefact of that limit, and the spend of 1.6 to 1.9 times B is not); (b) with any spend figure in its context (the notes in arm XA; the platform's `budget_usd` attachment in capped XC) workers stopped at 0.86 to 1.07 of B and handed back a partial list with a cost to finish. So the early hand-backs of wave 3 follow the information that the worker has spent its budget; the same stop happened without the hook's notes when the platform showed the spend. That is what compliance predicts; whether a worker at 0.86 with 14% left is "too early" cannot be told from these runs (the hand-backs name 66 to 76 unread files and "USD 0.25 to 0.30 more", which is more than 14%).

What this closes: at this task and budget (USD 0.30, 90 files, one worker type, sonnet) the notes did not make explorers stop earlier or deliver less than arm C by the protocol's measures, so the pre-registered rule gives "compliance" and no plugin change follows (the rule: a change follows only from "anxiety"). Outside that scope: tasks that fit the budget with room (the notes' effect on a worker that has plenty left), tasks far over the budget with a spend-blind control and enough turns (the uncapped XC ended at the turn limit, so how a spend-blind worker would end with more turns is not shown), and other worker types.
