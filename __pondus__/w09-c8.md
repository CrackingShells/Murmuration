## What the score is (checked in the rows, `rescore.md`)

- Score = log P(first token begins BLOCK) minus log P(first token begins ALLOW), summed over the top-20 token logprobs of the first generated token (`score_from` in `__instrumenta__/w09i_judge.py`). Higher means more block-like; refusals are the positive class. Recomputed from the stored `pb`, `pa`: difference 0 over 279 rows. The model's verdict equals `score > 0` in 279 of 279 rows (and the generated word equals the verdict in 385 of 385 rows). The top-20 holds 99.8% of the first-token mass (median; minimum 0.949; 11.5% of rows below 0.99), so the score is not a full-vocabulary quantity. No non-finite qwen score.
- Granite's score is log P(yes) minus log P(no) at the token after `<score>` (its first generated token is `<think>`); same polarity check (verdict equals `score > 0`: 279 of 279). Gemma has 6 and 9 infinite scores (BLOCK outside the top 20); not used in the claim.

## Weighting and interval

- Natural allows carry weight N_h/m_h (sum 19,243; minimum 2.0, maximum 222.1; 33 strata). Kish effective n of the 212 is 143.5. Refusals weigh 1 each, 41 sessions. AUC is a rank-based weighted Mann-Whitney with ties one half (my own implementation, sorting plus cumulative weights; it gives the explorer's 0.788 and the flipped-sign sanity 0.212).
- AUC 0.788 (unweighted 0.766). 95% intervals from three recipes: session-cluster bootstrap with the explorer's recipe 0.717 to 0.855 (the explorer's own run 0.709 to 0.854; the draw order of sessions differs, a Monte Carlo difference of 0.01); session-cluster, 2,000 draws, 0.717 to 0.852; refusals by session and allows resampled within stratum, 2,000 draws, 0.723 to 0.847. So 0.71 to 0.86 covers all of them.
- Weight sensitivity: weights capped at 50 give 0.777, at 20 give 0.776, each stratum equal 0.765, the 5 heaviest cases dropped 0.783.
- Granite 0.565 (0.476 to 0.658 across recipes); qwen full minus Granite +0.223 (paired session bootstrap 0.136 to 0.299, qwen higher in 100% of 2,000 draws). qwen full minus qwen short +0.053 (0.004 to 0.102); minus gemma short +0.031 (-0.033 to +0.098, not separated).

- ESTIMATE formula: share = TPR x 67 / (TPR x 67 + FPR x 19,243). It treats the 67 refusals (2026-07 to 2026-10) and the 19,243 natural allows (2026-10-08 to 2026-10-25, per w09-c7) as one population; the windows differ, so it is a rough order of magnitude. Taking only the 23 refusals of the allow-label era against the 19,243 allows, the share at 10% is 0.6%. It does not depend on the AUC being measured well; it is arithmetic on the table.
- "Far from a replacement" in numbers: no threshold catches 90% of refusals below 49% false blocks; at 10% false blocks more than half of the refusals (36 of 67) are allowed; and at the verdict's own threshold a user would see about 5,000 blocks per 45 recorded refusals caught in this frame.
- Held-out thresholds (threshold picked on training sessions for 10% weighted FPR, applied to held-out sessions, pooled, 20 seeds each): 5-fold TPR 44.5% (40.3 to 47.8%), FPR 10.2% (8.1 to 11.9%); 10-fold TPR 44.4%, FPR 10.4%. So the in-sample 46.3% at 10% barely moves out of sample; the FPR carries over within 2 points.
- Where it is weakest: natural allows flagged risky (80 cases, 24% of weight) AUC 0.635 against 0.836 on plain ones (132); Bash-only AUC 0.783, non-Bash 0.827; refusals of the allow-label era 0.818 against 0.771 earlier.

## Model re-run (spot check; the only model run of this claim)

- 45 cases (15 refusals, 30 natural allows; random, fixed seed), same server build, same GGUF, same prompt: verdicts equal 45 of 45. Scores are not identical: fresh minus stored mean -0.001, sd 0.284, maximum absolute difference 0.84 (15 of 45 above 0.25, all allows). I did not find the cause (prompt-cache state and batch size differ between the runs; not tested). Simulated Gaussian noise of that size on every score leaves the AUC at 0.785 (0.774 to 0.795) but moves the TPR at an in-sample 10% FPR to 43.9% (35.8 to 52.2%), so the second digit of any operating-point figure is noise.
- Cold first call 38.7 s with the rules prefix uncached, later calls median 0.96 s here (stored run: p50 0.72 s). Both agree with the explorer's latency to within what a different load on the machine explains.

## Reading

The explorer's central numbers reproduce from its rows by an independent implementation of the AUC and of the intervals, and the polarity is as stated. What the rows support: the rules help (qwen full is above Granite with the whole paired interval above 0, and above qwen short with the interval touching 0.004); what they do not support is use as a recorder or filter of the kind auto mode is: the best operating point at 10% false blocks still misses more than half of the recorded refusals and, in this frame, would flag about 60 allowed calls per refusal found.
