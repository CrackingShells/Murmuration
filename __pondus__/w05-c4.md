## Results

### m1: selftests and replay

Selftests of `budget.py` and `handback.py` PASS at ede2770 (the replay-hash check of the hand-written JSON went with the JSON). Replay of the 291 worker runs, old = 4c675ec, new = ede2770 (`__reports__/wave-05/author-w05-3/replay_merged_291.txt`): **285 of 291 equal, 6 differ**. The six (`replay_explain.txt`) differ in one way only: the old version sends a message (an over-budget note, or a block) a second time for a request count it already messaged, 1, 8, 2, 9, 1 and 2 times; with those repeats dropped from the old version's answers and log, answers, log and hand-back files are equal in all six. The cause is how the replay builds batches: it fires one PostToolBatch per tool-result record, and a request with two parallel calls has two records (the transcripts show `A, A, U, U`), so the old version sees two batches; the platform fires one PostToolBatch per batch (w03-c1), where the old version would send one message and the new version sends one (the counter `sent` in the state is the request count). The new version also says the message at the first PostToolUse of a batch, not after the last, so its time figure can be seconds earlier on a parallel batch. The list of 291 is 197 distinct transcript files (94 names repeat in `replay_transcripts_291.txt`, from overlapping glob patterns in w04-c5), so "291" counts replay runs, not transcripts; I did not know this when I filed. A replay with true batches (reproducer-w05-4: one PostToolBatch per assistant request, after its last result) gives 261 of 291 equal, 21 distinct transcripts differing, 11 of them with a message from the NEW build that the old one does not send (a note at the first PostToolUse of a batch that also holds the hand-back call, or after a hand-back deny, where the old build sees the batch end and the closed run). So the repeats on one request count are not the only difference, and the claim's explanation above covers the per-record replay only. Whether a build that answers at the last PostToolUse of a request (counting the request's calls in the transcript) could match the merged sequence was not tested; parallel PostToolUse hooks run concurrently and the order of completion is not known to a hook, so I have no argument that it can or cannot. My earlier sentence that no build removing the PostToolBatch start could meet m1 is withdrawn: it holds for the literal per-event output only.

## Decision (E7, as pre-registered)

- m1, "identical on 291 of 291 with all selftests passing": selftests pass; replay identical on 285 of 291 runs (197 distinct files) with one PostToolBatch per tool-result record, 261 of 291 with true batches (reproducer-w05-4): **not met**. (Reading m1 as "identical but for a message the old version repeats on one request count" it is met only in the per-record replay; with true batches it is not.)
- m2, "down by at least 50%": 52.5% at the interpolated price (51.8% short, 61.9% long), interval 44 to 59%: **met at its edge**.
- m3, "not slower than main at any payload size": a worker on the short transcript at 500 KB is 1.06 of main: **not met** (met at 2 KB and 200 KB, and for the main thread and the long-transcript worker at all three sizes).
- m4: **holds** (1 of 1).

The rule says "proposed if m1 ... and m2 ... and m3 ... and m4": two of four are not met, so **the build is not proposed** and it is set aside on the branch tip, as the brief asked: commit 0d3c879 puts `hooks/` and `scripts/` back to main's 4c675ec (`git diff 4c675ec HEAD -- hooks scripts` is empty). The build stays at ede2770; revert 0d3c879 to take it. The set-aside also undoes an accident: the w04-c5 lean code had been back on the branch tip since baba44f ("w05-c3: tools allowlist for research-pi"), which took the staged revert of 4c46fe4 that I had begun into its commit (the index is shared by the agents of this worktree).

What the PI may weigh beyond the rule (not decided here): the m1 criterion cannot be met by any build that removes the PostToolBatch start, which is the one change that removes a start per tool call; the per-wave saving is 127 serial seconds of about 88 minutes (ESTIMATE, wave-4 counts) at this machine's load, and the saving per start of a worker is small (0.83 to 0.62 at 1 KB, 0.97 at eight at once). A build that keeps main's two PostToolUse and PostToolBatch registrations but gates both in sh would meet m1 by construction and save the main thread's 193 starts only.

## What this closes, and what it does not

Closes: whether the check can move from PostToolBatch to PostToolUse, one start per tool call, with the ceiling hand-back kept and the notes delivered: it can (m4c, 1 of 1; replay equal up to repeats on one request count), and it cuts the starts of a wave by 45% and the serial hook seconds by 52% on this machine, with E7's rule not met on m1 and m3 and met at its edge on m2. Outside that scope: a call that fails (PostToolUse does not fire for 13 of 172 calls in w03-c1; the old PostToolBatch covered them; a `PostToolUseFailure` registration is untested), a pipe instead of a socket, a quiet machine (every wall figure here is inflated by the load noted in the scope), a platform that sends payloads above 31 KB, and `user_words.py` and `resume_brief.py` (started as `python3 <script>`, untouched).
