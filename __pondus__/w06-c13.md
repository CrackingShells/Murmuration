- Supported: under five parallel lab-like sessions the guard stayed under 7.7 s in 211 of 211 starts; the headroom to the 10 s limit is 2.4 s at the observed max, 1.3 times.
- Not supported either way: whether a timeout blocks a harmless command in normal load. 0 of 211 allows a one-sided 95 percent bound of about 1.4 percent per start (rule of three), so a rare timeout cannot be excluded, and the five-way load is not measured against the lab's real peak (this wave ran about ten agents).
- A timeout block is friction, not harm: the agent sees a hook failure and retries. Raising the limit costs nothing on the normal path (the limit is not a wait); it lengthens only a true hang, which nothing observed. My reading: the evidence does not require a higher timeout; it does not show the present one is too high a risk either; if the PI wants a hedge, a 20 s limit costs nothing measurable, and a ten-session version of h5 would settle it. That is an engineering choice, not one the data forces.
