3b · The cache-lifetime decision: 5 minutes or 1 hour (C)

A 1-hour entry costs more to write (USD 4.00 per million tokens for Sonnet, 8.00 for Opus; a 5-minute entry 2.50 and 5.00) and reads at the same price. It pays only when a pause falls between 5 minutes and 1 hour. The break-even is arithmetic: 1 hour wins when more than 0.385 of the tokens written are re-writes (CITED prices, the lab's price table). Wave 8 redid wave 7's figure on timed pauses only (waves 5–7: 14 authors, 14 verifiers, 9 explorers, 3 PI sessions; wave 4 has no recorded pauses). Net per life is what 1 hour would save over 5 minutes in one agent's life: right of zero, 1 hour pays.

The line is the 10th to 90th percentile of the lives of that type, the tick the median, the dot the mean. Workers share one axis (USD −0.4 to +0.4); the PI has its own (n = 3 sessions: +13.1, +30.7, −0.16, so it says little about the next one). ESTIMATE; the model reproduces the recorded cache cost within 0.2–4% (CHECKED, waves 5–6).

Which rule picks what

Reproducers and explorers: 5 minutes under almost every rule. The PI: 1 hour under every rule. Authors are the knife-edge: the average-cost rules (expected value, token share 0.372 against 0.385, probability of gain) say 5 minutes, by about USD 0.02 a life; the tail rules (CVaR, minimax regret) say 1 hour, because 1 hour costs a small certain premium and 5 minutes risks a rare loss of 0.7–1.0 USD. That is a choice of attitude, not a measurement: more data of the same kind will not settle it. (The report counts six rules, 4 to 2; the figure shows seven rows, 5 to 2, because it keeps the probability row apart.) The lab keeps authors on 1 hour by the PI's decision, with no claim; w07-c7, which made the earlier case, is escalated. Every long pause that costs a worker money (10 of 13 misses) follows a hand-back: the author waits to be resumed (22 hand-backs, median wait 161 s). Switching per phase is not shown possible in Claude Code (the agent file fixes it per dispatch) and would be worth at most USD 3.78 over the 14 authors' lives.

3b · Is a 1-hour cache worth it? The break-even by agent type

A 1-hour cache entry costs more to write (USD 4.00 per million tokens for Sonnet, against 2.50 for a 5-minute one) and the same to read (0.10). It pays only when a worker pauses for more than 5 minutes and less than an hour, because then the 5-minute entry has expired and the whole prefix must be written again. Per worker, let W be every token it wrote to cache and R the prefix tokens it had to write again after such pauses. With a 1-hour cache it saves R × (2.50 − 0.10) and pays (W − R) × (4.00 − 2.50) more, so it is worth it exactly when R / W > 1.5 / 3.9 = 0.385. That threshold is arithmetic on the prices; R and W come from the 47 workers of waves 4–6 (__instrumenta__/w07_ttl_by_type.py).

5 · Where a worker's time goes

The time shares are over the workers of waves 5–6. The record set behind the page is waves 4–6: 47 workers, 2,633 requests. Shares are read from the records (MEASURED). The hook and classifier shares are ESTIMATE. The costs below them each use their own base. Wave 8 adds one larger than all of them: a worker's own earlier turns, 41.6% of its context USD (see 3).

Why the two largest of these cost money

Every model request sends the whole conversation again: system prompt, tool list, every message and every tool result so far. Anthropic's prompt cache keeps the processed prefix, so a request that repeats it pays the cache-read price for those tokens instead of the input price. For Sonnet, the workers' model, the lab's price table gives USD 2.00 per million input tokens, 0.10 per million read from cache, 2.50 per million written to a 5-minute cache entry and 4.00 to a 1-hour entry (scripts/lab_status.py, PRICES). A cache entry lives 5 minutes from its last use for a subagent and 1 hour for the main thread; each hit renews it.

why it costs

Cache re-writes after gaps over 5 minutes

Why it costs: when a worker makes no request for more than 5 minutes (one long tool call, or a wait for a message), its cache entry expires. The next request must write the whole context again at 2.50 instead of reading it at 0.10, 25 times the price. Example: a 150,000-token context costs about USD 0.015 to read and 0.375 to re-write. Over waves 4–6, 23 such re-writes cost USD 10.41 above a read.

What the lab controls: the gaps, not the prices. No tool call waits more than 4½ minutes (in the agent files since wave 5); authors now keep 1-hour entries (w07-c7). An author resumed by a verifier after a long wait is the case that remains: 10 of 13 worker misses follow a hand-back wait (explorer-w08-c). Wave 8 puts re-writes after pauses at 15.7% of worker context USD, USD 13.12 (explorer-w08-a, ESTIMATE).

why it costs

Tool results over 5,000 characters

Why it costs: a tool result stays in the context for the rest of the worker's life, so every later request reads it again from cache. The cost is its size × the number of later requests × 0.10 per million tokens. Example: one Read of 61,427 characters (about 25,000 tokens) was re-read by 122 later requests, about 3 million cached tokens, USD 0.31. 5,000 characters is the bucket the probe used, not a limit of the platform; the cost grows smoothly with size and with how early in a worker's life the result comes.

What the lab controls: the size and the timing: scripts that print a summary, Reads with an offset and a limit, a large result read late rather than early, or read by a short-lived helper that hands back a summary. Wave 8 measured the ceiling (explorer-w08-a, ESTIMATE): capping every tool result at 5,000 tokens saves at most 7.0% of worker context USD, at 2,500 tokens 15.6%; lab-script output alone 0.5% and lab documents 6.9% at 2,500. It is a ceiling: nothing needed was cut and no re-read followed.

So the prices and the 5-minute lifetime are the platform's. What a run does with them is not: both costs are set by the shape of the agent loop (how long it pauses, how much it reads and when), which the plugin's text and scripts can change. The mechanism here is from Anthropic's prompt-caching documentation as the lab has used it since wave 2, not fetched again this wave.
