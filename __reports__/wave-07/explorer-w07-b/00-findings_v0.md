# Where a worker's wall time goes: the agent loop, timed from the transcripts of waves 4-6 (findings v0)

Stayed inside the brief. Two scope notes. (1) Three transcript sets: wave 4 (`c533f1c6`, 15 workers), wave 5 (`6b0e1f2a`, 15 workers), wave 6 (`74fa512e` in the `blissful-villani` folder, 17 workers); 47 worker transcripts, 2,633 requests, 2,883 tool calls, USD 65.75 at list price. Wave 4's transcripts have no `api-request` records, so there model time and client gap cannot be told apart (they are one number, `pre`). (2) Per-call hook durations are not in these records for PreToolUse/PostToolUse successes, so "hook time per turn" is only what the records hold (SubagentStart, PostToolBatch, one cancelled PreToolUse). Labels: MEASURED (counts and timings from records), ESTIMATE (formula given), CONJECTURE, CITED. Sums of "wall" are sums over workers that ran in parallel, not wave time.

## Result

MEASURED: a worker's loop is model requests and tool phases, not hooks. Over waves 5-6 (32 workers, 56,436 s summed wall) the model request is 30.8% of wall, the tool phase up to 120 s 20.1%, tool phases over 120 s (headless runs, `timeout`, `until` loops) 25.5%, idle waits for a message 22.3%, and the client gap from a tool result to the next request 1.3% (median 0.30-0.34 s). Without the idle waits and long phases the loop proper is 59% model, 38.5% tool, 2.5% gap; a request cycle costs a median 5.7-6.1 s of model time plus 1.5 s of tool phase plus 0.3 s of gap. Forty-one percent of the model time of waves 5-6 is thinking (recorded `thinkingDurationMs`: 7,074 s of 17,355 s). The hook time the records do show (SubagentStart 90.9 s over 47 workers, PostToolBatch 9.9 s over 44 records) is 0.13% of wall.

MEASURED: 92.0% of worker requests that carry a tool call carry exactly one (2,414 of 2,625); 16.3% of calls go out in a multi-call message; the PI 81-90%. ESTIMATE: independent calls could have saved little. A heuristic over the transcripts finds 67 requests (2.5%) that could have merged under a loose rule and 32 (1.2%) under a strict one; I judged 62 pairs by hand (precision 53-63% loose, 62-78% strict), so the audited range is about 20-42 requests of 2,633 (0.8-1.6%), 120-290 s of 76,439 s summed wall (0.2-0.4%) and USD 0.2-0.4 of 65.75 (0.3-0.6%). The reason is visible in the records: 95% of Bash calls already chain two or more statements (mean 4.7), and 75% chain three or more, so the model batches inside one shell command; what stays serial is looking at a result, then choosing the next file or line range.

MEASURED, bigger than hooks and batching: the classifier-decided Bash call. The tool phase of a trivial single Bash call (`ls`, `cat`, `head`, `git`, `sed`, ... with a small result) has median 1.49 s (n = 1,159, p10 1.05 s); a Read has 0.16 s (n = 9 single, 0.17 s over 108 calls). The gap, 1.3 s per call, times 2,348 classifier-decided Bash calls is about 3,100 s over all three waves (4.1% of summed wall); for waves 5-6 alone, 1,715 calls x 1.33 s = 2,280 s, 7.8% of the loop proper (ESTIMATE: it contains the classifier, the PreToolUse and PostToolUse hooks, the shell start and the result record; the records cannot split them; the earlier measured classifier is 0.45-0.75 s per call, so 0.6-0.9 s of it is unexplained, compare "0.85 s per Bash call" in `__statera__/wave-05.md` line 94, CITED).

The other cost lines that exceed the hooks, all MEASURED with ESTIMATE formulas below: cache re-writes after gaps over 300 s (23 misses, USD 10.41 over a read, 15.8% of workers' spend, 9 of them after one long tool call); retries after errors (160 requests, 6.8% of wave 6's USD, from the harness refusing `sleep N` chains and the worktree-isolation refusal of complex git commands); tool outputs of 5,000+ characters re-read by every later request (29.7% of the workers' cache-read USD, USD 10.30 of 34.59).

## Worst-fitting case

The story that fits best: the loop is requests (model time plus thinking) and a classifier-heavy Bash phase; hooks are 0.13% of wall; calls go out one per message (92%).

Fits worst, in three parts.

1. Batching does not pay, although 92% of messages carry one call. The pairs the rule counts mostly are the model looking at a listing or a `grep -n` and then reading the file or line range it chose (in the 62 pairs I read, 18 were N and 8 M). The best-fitting worker for batching is reproducer-w05-3 (32 requests, 35 calls, 436 s, 5 loose / 3 strict absorbed requests: 16% / 9% of its requests); the typical worker (reproducer median 32 requests, 669 s) saves 1-2 requests; the saving over all 47 workers is below 1.6% at best. [source: `per_worker.tsv`, `audit_judgments.md`]
2. The worker that fits "the loop costs the time" worst is author-w04-2 (107 requests, 115 calls, wall 4,599 s, USD 3.62): 2,893 s (63%) of its wall is long tool phases (headless runs under `timeout`), model plus gap 1,384 s (30%), batching saves 2 requests. A second worst case, author-w06-3 (87 requests, wall 6,365 s, USD 2.70): 4,281 s of its wall is waiting for a message (one gap of 4,172 s), model 787 s, tool 308 s plus 964 s long. For authors, long tool phases (31.3% of wall) and idle waits (25.6%) outweigh model time (18.2%) and the loop's per-request costs. [source: `per_worker.tsv`, `tables.md` "by agent type"]
3. The classifier reading rests on a differential (Bash 1.49 s minus Read 0.16 s), not on a record of the classifier call. The 17 trivial single Bash calls decided by mode or rule (no classifier) have a median 1.03 s (p10 0.20 s, p90 31 s); n is too small to say the classifier is the difference. [source: `tables.md` "trivial single Bash calls"]

## Not covered

- The classifier's own duration and the per-call PreToolUse/PostToolUse hook durations: no record holds them in these transcripts (the permission fields are ids and decisions). The 1.3 s differential is all-in. A headless probe with a trivial Bash call, a Read, and the hooks switched on and off in the same session would split it; I started no `claude` run.
- Independence of calls was judged by a text heuristic and two hand samples (n = 30, 32, one reader); decision dependence (the model chose call 2 because of result 1) is invisible to it, so the savings are an upper bound of what a model could do without behaving differently. No batching was tried live.
- Wave 4 has no request timestamps: its model time and client gap are one number, and idle waits there follow a rule (message arrived and gap over 60 s).
- Output tokens in the records are lower bounds, so "output USD" of absorbed requests and any per-token generation rate are not reported; the thinking share comes from `thinkingDurationMs`, which wave 4 lacks.
- The wave-6 PI transcript is the `blissful-villani` session; the wave-5 session also holds the wave-6 opening (its wall includes the human-away gap). Waves 1-3 (`ab96c5ed`, 44 workers) were not read.
- Tool concurrency inside one message is inferred from result times, not from a record of start times.
- USD is list price of the tokens in the records; auto mode's classifier cost is not in them.
