# The idle PI: what it costs to wake, what it did while waiting, what a fresh session saves (findings v0)

Stayed inside the brief. Two scope notes. (1) Both PI sessions already were one session per wave: wave 4's started at the wave's opening (request 0 at 75k tokens), wave 5's started at wave 5's opening (74,966 tokens); only the wave-6 opening was added to wave 5's session. So "one fresh session per wave" saves nothing for waves 4 and 5; I priced the one continuation turn and, for the case where it does matter, the waves 1-3 session (explorer-w05-b's table). (2) Headless-run spend is not in any transcript folder I read; I quote it from the wave notes (CITED).

Labels: MEASURED, ESTIMATE, CONJECTURE, CITED. Prices (Opus 5.5 USD per Mtok: input 4, output 20, cache read 0.20, 5-minute write 5, 1-hour write 8; Sonnet 5.5: 2, 10, 0.10, 2.5, 4) are the table in `scripts/lab_status.py` PRICES. I re-checked them against the Anthropic prompt-caching pricing page through context7 (`/websites/platform_claude_en`, source URL https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching, queried 2026-10-21): the Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 rows match (CITED, via context7; I did not fetch the page itself). Auto mode's permission-classifier cost is in no record (explorer-w05-f), so every USD below is a lower bound.

## Result

MEASURED: the PI was idle 77.0% of wave 4 and 73.0% of wave 5 (turn time against the span from first to last turn), and that idle time costs no tokens; the PI's money goes into 24 and 23 wake-ups, 68% and 70% of its spend being reactive (woken by a worker), each wake-up re-reading a 200-390k context. The PI was 19.1% (wave 4) and 25.9% (wave 5) of each wave's measured spend including headless runs, 37.0% and 42.9% of PI plus subagents alone. Wake-ups that changed no state (text-only or read-only) were 6 of 24 (USD 0.51, 4.8% of PI spend) in wave 4 and 9 of 23 (USD 0.92, 7.8%) in wave 5, so "fewer wake-ups" is worth at most about USD 0.5-0.9 a wave; "one fresh session per wave" is already the practice and a fresh session for the wave-6 opening would have saved USD 0.28 to lose 1.43 depending on how much handoff it must read (ESTIMATE). The evidence for "the PI should use its waits" is narrower and concrete: wave 4's PI wrote all 101 puzzle dispositions in one pass at the end although workers had been handing back since 00:38 UTC (puzzle files landing meanwhile; the count of 101 is the PI's own text), and wave 5's PI did a block of its own work (edits to four skill files, claims w05-c8/c9/c10) at 06:59 UTC whose inputs, for w05-c9, had been complete since 06:36 UTC, 23 minutes earlier, with the PI idle in between (ESTIMATE of what was movable, MEASURED for the times).

## Worst-fitting case

The story that fits best: a PI with 73-77% idle time that spends about a fifth to a quarter of the wave and could use its waits. The case that fits it worst, in three parts.

1. Idle time costs nothing in tokens; the cost is wake-ups, and 6 to 7.8% of PI spend is the part that changed nothing. Cost per wake-up grows with context only modestly (per request 0.085 to 0.106 from 225k to 325k) and mostly follows how many requests a wake-up needs (2.6 to 4.2 at similar context) [source: `summary_tables.txt`].
2. When the wave-5 PI did use a wait for its own work, the verifier found errors in it: the reassessment times in the wave note were guesses that drifted up to 1 h 40 min from the commit times (w05-c9 OVERSTATED), and it needed 3 PI turns of repair and a restated claim [source: `__statera__/wave-05.md` Reassessments (16:03 note), Dispatches row reproducer-w05-8]. Own work is not free: it adds context for every later request and adds a verifier to the wave.
3. The best-fitting single case against "fewer wake-ups": the 22 wave-5 hand-back wake-ups include 5 `message` and 5 `dispatch` turns that moved verifiers within seconds of an author's hand-back (turn 7 of wave 5: 11 s to dispatch a verifier). A PI that batches its wake-ups delays those; explorer-w05-a measured the PI's response lag as a median 4 s with no worker held up more than 59 s, and wave 4's critical path was 78.8 min of one author's headless cells, so batching would not have changed wave length either way (CITED: `__reports__/wave-05/explorer-w05-a/00-findings_v0.md`).

Also the case that breaks the wave-6 note's "five times": the read part is 3.9 times (75k against 390k), but a whole request is 1.4-1.9 times, because output and the new cache write do not depend on the context.

This costs no extra request at a higher context; the PI wakes anyway.
