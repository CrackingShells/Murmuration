No new symbol. Terms used only in this file and in `__pondus__/w01-c5.md`: *turn* (a distinct assistant `message.id` of one agent file), *segment* (the turns between two wake-ups of the same agent), *gap* (seconds from the agent's last record to its wake-up), *cold resume* (the first turn after a wake-up writes more than half of its context to the cache).

## S-w01-author-w01-5-3
A resumed subagent pays a cold cache when the gap passes about 5 minutes, and in the research loop that is the normal case for the author. The verifier reads the claim, runs the quick command and a sample (minutes), writes its independent result, and only then messages the author. So each verifier question wakes the author after a gap well above 5 minutes and re-writes the author's whole context at the 5-minute write price: for a 300k-token author that is about 0.69 USD per exchange at the Sonnet 5.5 list price, more than the author's own cost of answering (0.1 to 0.25 USD for 2 to 3 turns). The resumes I measured at 425 s or more (5 of 12) all re-wrote 81 to 87 percent of their context. I have not measured a verifier-to-author exchange (there is none in the data yet; this author's resume by reproducer-w01-5 will be the first, and its first-turn cache write will show it). The design-session probe agents were resumed after 3.2 and 12.9 s and stayed warm, so they say nothing about this case.

## S-w01-author-w01-5-6
The platform calls a limit stop "completed" and delivers the PI's resume text to the stopped agent as "The coordinator sent a message while you were working". Both are wrong in a way that matters to a parser: the notification `<status>completed</status>` is the same for a clean finish and a limit stop, and only the summary text ("stopped at its 60-turn limit (partial result; SendMessage to task-id to continue)") separates them. The agent's own file carries no marker (explorer-w01-a made the same point). A ledger or hook that counts statuses will count a stopped explorer as a success. The turn count (60) is the only record in the file itself that a limit was involved, and only if one knows the limit.
refs: `__reports__/reflective_run/w01/author-w01-5/stops.csv` column `summary`; main transcript records 291 and 293 (notice), 306 and 307 (resume)

## S-w01-author-w01-5-7
My first PI-reaction table was wrong, and a verifier's re-run caught it. It reported that 15 to 18 percent of notices in genesis and design were answered after more than 300 s, and I turned that into an expected cold-cache cost of about 0.10 USD per stop. The table counted every `<task-notification>`, background-shell notices included, and `--until` did not bound the reaction message. Restricted to notices about workers the PI launched, genesis has 11 notices with a maximum wait of 9.6 s and design 12 distinct ones (my second version counted 22, because the design session contains the forked genesis history and repeats its notices; reproducer-w01-5 found this) with a maximum of 33.7 s; with this run's 9 that is 32 distinct worker notices, none above 300 s. What the first table measured was mostly how long the PI took to answer a finished `sleep` or a background command, usually while waiting for the user. The puzzle that remains: the platform reports background-shell completions through the same `<task-notification>` channel as worker completions, with no field I found other than the task id to tell them apart, so any ledger or hook that counts notices will mix them. The same blind spot would hit a `SubagentStop`-free design that reads notifications.
