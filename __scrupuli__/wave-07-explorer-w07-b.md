## S-w07-explorer-w07-b-2
Workers send one call per message in 92% of the cases and yet batching saves under 1.6% at best, because the model already puts a mean 4.7 statements into one Bash call (75% have 3 or more) and the next call usually depends on seeing the listing. So "parallel-first" at the message level is already done inside the shell, serially (`;`, `&&`), on one core of the shell and with one classifier call. Nobody has measured whether a long chain is cheaper or dearer than the same statements as parallel tool calls (several Bash calls in one message ran concurrently here, slowest minus fastest result a median 0.70 s, but each Bash call carries its own classifier call). The records cannot say; a paired probe could.

## S-w07-explorer-w07-b-3
The idle wait is 22.3% of the workers' wall in waves 5-6 (12,601 s; author-w06-3 alone 4,281 s in one 4,172 s gap), and wave 4's PI sat idle 77% while its workers waited on peers. These are workers or PIs blocked on a message, the cache expires (15.8% of worker spend is cache re-writes after gaps over 300 s) and nobody counts the wait as work. I did not find what the worker with the 4,172 s gap was waiting for (a message to arrive, an author's answer to a verifier question, a background task); the record shows a result, then 70 minutes, then a request with a queued message. Which of the three it is changes whether the fix is the PI's dispatch order, the exchange protocol, or the worker's way of waiting.
